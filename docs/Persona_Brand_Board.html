4 · Reference apps worth stealing ideas from

  • Things 3 — the most beautiful todo app in its category; study its calm spacing and "Today / Upcoming" views.
  • Due — nagging reminders that repeat until you act. This is basically your strict-reminder feature done commercially.
  • Structured — a visual timeline for planning a single day; great model for your calendar + planner day view.
  • TickTick / Todoist — natural-language task entry ("meeting tomorrow 8am") like your voice parser.

5 · Samsung apps style glassmorphism — his call (NOT Good Lock)

  • Frosted glass surfaces — translucent headers, sheets and cards with backdrop blur over the paper background. Layered, airy, soft.
  • Soft color presentation — Samsung's airy pastels and gentle gradient washes; color in glows, never harsh flats.
  • Big collapsing headers — large bold screen title that shrinks into the toolbar on scroll; content lives in the thumb zone.
  • Squircle cards, hairline dividers — generous rounded corners, whisper-thin separators, soft shadows.
  • Pill bottom nav — floating, charcoal, active-section highlight (awwwards and Samsung agree here).
  • Springy micro-motion — sheets slide up with spring, toggles bounce softly, list items rise + fade staggered. Nothing linear or harsh.
  • Segmented controls + big switches — for reminder type (once / strict), planner ranges, music filters.

Rule of thumb: if a screen would look at home in Samsung Clock, Calendar or SmartThings — frosted, soft, light — it's right. Good Lock's playful customization style is explicitly OUT.
